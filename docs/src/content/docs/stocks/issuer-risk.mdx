A tokenized stock isn't fully yours the way SOL is. Its issuer keeps powers over every
holder's position. Heirloom can't remove them. It shows them, and checks them again
whenever a recovery or claim would move anything.

## What issuers can do

| Power | xStocks | Ondo |
|---|---|---|
| **Clawback:** move tokens out of any account | Yes | No |
| **Pause:** stop all transfers of the stock | Yes | Yes |
| **Freeze:** lock one account | Yes | Yes |
| **Add a transfer hook later** | Yes | Yes |

The dashboard tags each stock with these, and gives each issuer a risk tier.

## What happens at recovery

| Situation | Result | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The stock is **paused** | `AssetPaused` | Retry once the issuer unpauses it |
| The account is **frozen** | `AccountFrozen` | Only the issuer can unfreeze it |
| A **clawback power was added** after coverage began | `PermanentDelegateAdded` | The payout won't go ahead on changed terms |
| A **transfer hook was switched on** | `TransferHookUnsupported` | Not supported yet |

Withdrawing from your own vault is less strict: a pause or freeze still blocks it, but a
newly added clawback power doesn't. A change the issuer makes should never trap you.

## What Heirloom can't see

Mergers, delistings and ticker changes happen off-chain. If an issuer retires a stock,
what happens to holders is between them and the issuer.

## Unsupported issuers

Some issuers freeze every new account and only unfreeze approved holders. A vault or
recovery wallet could never be sure of receiving those stocks, so Heirloom won't cover
them.
