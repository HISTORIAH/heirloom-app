A tokenized stock is not a bearer asset in the way SOL is. Its issuer keeps powers over every
holder's position, written into the mint as Token-2022 extensions. Heirloom can't remove
those powers; it shows them, and it re-checks them at the moment a recovery or claim would
move anything.

## What the issuers can do

As read from their mints on mainnet:

| Power | Extension | xStocks | Ondo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clawback** — move tokens out of any account, without the holder | Permanent delegate | Yes | No |
| **Pause** — halt every transfer of the stock | Pausable | Yes | Yes |
| **Freeze** — lock an individual account | Freeze authority | Yes | Yes |
| **Hook slot** — switch on a transfer hook later, without a new mint | Transfer hook, unset | Yes | Yes |

Each of these appears as a tag next to the stock on the dashboard. The registry also gives
every issuer a **risk tier**, higher meaning more control over holders' funds.

Ondo's lack of a permanent delegate is a real difference: nobody but the holder, or a
delegate the holder approved, can move an Ondo stock out of an account.

## What happens at recovery

Nothing checked when a stock was covered is trusted later, because the issuer can change the
mint while a plan is live. A recovery or a claim re-checks, and stops with a specific reason:

| Situation | Result | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The issuer has **paused** the stock | `AssetPaused` | Wait. It can be retried once the issuer unpauses it |
| The issuer has **frozen** the account | `AccountFrozen` | Only the issuer can thaw it |
| A **permanent delegate appeared** after coverage began | `PermanentDelegateAdded` | The payout won't proceed on changed custody terms |
| The issuer **switched on a transfer hook** | `TransferHookUnsupported` | Not supported yet |

Withdrawing from your own vault is deliberately less strict. A pause or a frozen account
still blocks it — the token program would refuse the transfer anyway — but a newly added
permanent delegate does not. You should never be trapped by a change the issuer made.

## What Heirloom can't see

Mergers, delistings, and ticker changes are decided off-chain by the issuer, and issuers'
terms say such adjustments may not be visible to holders. No program can react to them. If
an issuer retires a stock, what happens to holders is between them and the issuer.

## Issuers that aren't supported

Some tokenized securities start every new account **frozen** and only thaw accounts for
holders the issuer has approved. A vault account or a recovery wallet could never be relied
on to receive them, so Heirloom refuses to cover them.
