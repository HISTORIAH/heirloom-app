Tokenized stocks don't pay dividends in cash, and they don't mint new tokens for a split.
Both work through a **display multiplier** on the mint — the Token-2022 `ScaledUiAmount`
extension.

## How it works

Every stock has a raw balance, in whole token units, and a multiplier. What your wallet
shows is the raw balance times the multiplier:

```txt
raw balance        10.00000000
multiplier         1.0032690125
shown in a wallet  10.032690125
```

- A **dividend** nudges the multiplier up by the dividend's value, reinvested. Apple xStock
  sat at about 1.0033 after five dividends.
- A **split** multiplies it — 2 for a 2-for-1 split. A reverse split divides it.

Your raw balance never changes. The multiplier does, for every holder at once.

## Scheduled ahead of time

An issuer sets the next multiplier together with the moment it takes effect, before the
ex-date. That schedule is on-chain, so the dashboard's **upcoming corporate actions** list
reads it straight from the mint: which of your stocks will change, by how much, and when.

## Why Heirloom only works in raw units

Every amount Heirloom moves is a raw amount — recoveries, claims, deposits, withdrawals, and
fees. That keeps each transfer exact, whatever the multiplier is doing.

It is also why a backup covers a **share** of a holding rather than a fixed number of shares.
"10 AAPLx" would quietly mean something different after every dividend; "50% of the holding"
means the same thing forever. Amounts you type in the app are in the units your wallet shows,
and are converted back to raw units using the multiplier in force at that moment.
