Tokenized stocks like xStocks and Ondo Global Markets are tokens, so they can be lost
with a key like anything else. **Heirloom Stocks**, at `stocks.heirlm.xyz`, protects them
in one of two ways. It runs on its own program, `heirloom-stocks`, separate from the
audited estate program.

<Callout type="warning" title="Devnet only">
`heirloom-stocks` is on devnet. xStocks and Ondo only exist on mainnet, so devnet uses
test stocks set up like theirs. See [Addresses & clusters](/docs/reference/addresses/).
</Callout>

## Side by side

| | Backup mode | Vault mode |
|---|---|---|
| Where the stocks sit | Your wallet | An account the plan owns |
| Can you trade them | Yes | No, withdraw first |
| Who receives them | Your recovery wallet | Your heir |
| How much moves | A share you choose, 1% to 100% | Everything in the vault |
| Can another app break it | Yes, see [Delegate eviction](/docs/stocks/delegate-eviction/) | No |
| Fee | 0.75% on recovery | 0.75% on claim, 0.5% on early withdrawal |

You can run one of each: a backup for stocks you trade, and a vault for stocks you are
putting away.

## Backup mode

Your stocks stay in your wallet. Heirloom is approved to move them, but only after you
stop checking in and the grace period runs out. Then your recovery wallet can move the
covered share to itself. Your lost key is never needed.

The share applies to whatever you hold at recovery time, so it stays right as you trade.

## Vault mode

Your stocks move into a vault the plan owns. If you stop checking in, your heir claims
everything in it. Until then you can withdraw at any time.

## What both share

Both use the same check-in interval and grace period as an estate, and both can name a
guardian and a check-in signer. Changing a plan's settings does **not** count as a
check-in here, unlike an estate.

## Supported stocks

Only issuers Heirloom has registered: currently xStocks and Ondo. Stocks that start
frozen, can't be transferred, or have a live transfer hook are refused. See
[Issuer controls](/docs/stocks/issuer-risk/) for what issuers can still do.
