Tokenized stocks — xStocks, Ondo Global Markets — are SPL tokens, so they can be lost with
a key like any other. **Heirloom Stocks**, at `stocks.heirlm.xyz`, protects them in one of
two ways. Both run on their own program, `heirloom-stocks`, separate from the audited
estate program.

<Callout type="warning" title="Devnet only, today">
`heirloom-stocks` is deployed to **devnet**; its address is in
[Addresses & clusters](/docs/reference/addresses/). xStocks and Ondo mint their stocks only
on mainnet, so on devnet the supported stocks are test equities, configured like an xStock and an Ondo stock. Balances on devnet are test
balances.
</Callout>

## Backup mode

Your stocks **stay in your wallet**. Covering a holding approves your backup plan as the
token account's delegate, with an unlimited allowance. Nothing moves, so you can keep
trading the position or use it as collateral.

You name a **recovery wallet** — one you control and keep somewhere else. If you stop
checking in, and the grace period after that runs out too, the recovery wallet can move the
covered holdings to itself. It signs; your lost key is never needed.

- **Allocation.** Each holding is covered for a share of its balance, from 1% to 100%. The
  share is applied to whatever the balance is at recovery, so it never goes stale as you
  trade.
- **Fee.** 0.75% of what a recovery moves, paid to the treasury.
- **The catch.** A token account has only one delegate. Approving another app on the same
  account quietly replaces Heirloom — see [Delegate eviction](/docs/stocks/delegate-eviction/).

## Vault mode

Your stocks **move into a vault** the plan owns. If you stop checking in, your **heir**
claims everything in it. Until then you can withdraw at any time.

- **Everything goes.** A vault always passes on its whole balance. To leave part of a
  position, deposit only that part.
- **No eviction.** Nothing you approve elsewhere can displace a vault.
- **Fees.** 0.75% of a claim; 0.5% of an early withdrawal.
- **The catch.** Vaulted stocks can't be traded or used as collateral until you withdraw
  them.

## Side by side

| | Backup | Vault |
|---|---|---|
| Where the stocks sit | Your wallet | A plan-owned account |
| Tradeable while covered | Yes | No — withdraw first |
| Who receives them | Your recovery wallet | Your heir |
| How much moves | Your chosen share of each holding | Everything in the vault |
| Can another approval break it | Yes, and it has to be repaired | No |
| Fee | 0.75% on recovery | 0.75% on claim, 0.5% on early withdrawal |

You can run one of each at the same time: a backup for the stocks you trade, a vault for the
ones you are putting away.

## What both share

Both use the same check-in cycle as an estate — an interval, then a grace period — and both
support the same optional helpers:

- a **guardian**, who can push the deadline out once if you are alive but can't check in;
- a **check-in wallet**, a hot wallet allowed to check in for you and nothing else.

Changing a plan's settings never counts as a check-in, so a scheduled edit can't keep a plan
alive by accident.

## Which stocks are supported

Coverage is limited to issuers Heirloom has registered. The registry is keyed by an issuer's
**mint authority** rather than by individual mint — one xStocks key signs for every xStock,
and one Ondo key for every Ondo stock — so a single entry admits an issuer's whole catalogue,
including listings added later. Mints that start frozen by default, can't be transferred, or
have a live transfer hook are refused. [Issuer controls](/docs/stocks/issuer-risk/) explains
what the supported issuers can still do to a position.
