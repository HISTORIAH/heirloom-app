You need a Solana wallet, a little SOL for fees, and the address of the wallet that
should inherit. Everything below happens in [app.heirlm.xyz](https://app.heirlm.xyz/dashboard);
every step is one transaction you sign yourself.

<Callout type="note" title="You are on devnet">
The program is deployed to devnet. Set your wallet to devnet and get test SOL from a
faucet before you start. See [Addresses & clusters](/docs/reference/addresses/).
</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect a wallet">
    Open the app and hit **Connect Wallet**. The connected wallet becomes the estate's
    `authority` — the owner. It is the only wallet that can change settings, add assets,
    or withdraw early, so connect the one you actually want to own this.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name an heir">
    Go to **Create Estate**. Paste the address of the wallet that should inherit, and give
    the estate a label (up to 32 characters — it is stored on-chain and it is how you tell
    two estates apart).

    One estate has exactly one heir. If you want two people to inherit different things,
    open two estates. If you want a way back into your own funds, name a second wallet of
    your own — that is [wallet recovery](/docs/using/wallet-recovery/).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Deposit">
    Add SOL, SPL tokens, or both. Each token you pick is *registered* as a claimable
    asset, which creates its marker account and the vault's token account in the same
    transaction as the deposit.

    You can add more later, and you can always take everything back with an
    [emergency withdraw](/docs/using/emergency-withdraw/).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the clock">
    Three numbers, all of them yours to pick:

    - **Heartbeat interval** — how long you may go quiet before the estate is considered
      at risk. 30 days is a common choice.
    - **Grace period** — extra time after the interval lapses, before the heir can claim.
      This is the window where the app is warning you and nothing has happened yet.
    - **Pause duration** — how far a guardian may push the deadline out, once, if you name
      one.

    Each is capped at 365 days. Optionally register a **hot signer**: a second wallet of
    yours that is allowed to send check-ins and nothing else, so the cold key that owns the
    estate can stay offline.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and sign">
    The review step shows the exact estate that is about to exist. Signing sends one
    transaction that creates the estate, creates the vault, registers every asset you
    chose, and moves the deposits in.

    The clock starts at the moment the transaction lands.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check in">
    On the dashboard, press **Send heartbeat**. That writes the current time to
    `last_heartbeat` and pushes the whole deadline forward by your interval. It costs a
    signature and network fees — there is no protocol fee on a check-in.

    Do this whenever the dashboard says the window is closing. If you would rather not
    remember, use the hot signer from a phone wallet at [/heartbeat](https://app.heirlm.xyz/heartbeat).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you just built

<Code caption="After the quickstart">
```txt
estate    PDA   seeds ["estate", your_wallet, heir_wallet]   ← the rules
vault     PDA   seeds ["vault",  your_wallet, heir_wallet]   ← the assets
asset     PDA   seeds ["asset",  estate, mint]               ← one per token
```
</Code>

No private key exists for any of these. Your wallet is the only signer that can direct
the estate, and the program is the only thing that can move what is inside the vault.
