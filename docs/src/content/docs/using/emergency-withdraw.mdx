An emergency withdraw — `revoke` in the program — returns everything in the vault to the
authority's wallet and closes the estate.

It works in **every state before distribution**: active, grace, and claimable. There is no
window in which your own funds are out of reach, including after an estate has become
claimable but before the heir has signed.

## The fee

**0.5%** per asset, to the treasury. Lower than the claim fee on purpose: the exit that
gets your money back should never be the expensive one.

## Order matters

The SOL leg closes the vault, so it cannot run while token accounts are still open. The
program enforces it:

```rust
require!(estate.claimable_assets <= 1, HeirloomError::TokensFirst);
```

The app's `revokeAll` bundles token withdrawals first and the SOL withdrawal last into a
single transaction. Calling the program directly, you have to do the same.

## Partial withdrawals

`revoke` operates per asset. Pulling one token out does not close the estate — the estate
and vault survive until the SOL leg runs and `claimable_assets` reaches zero. So you can
use it to remove a single asset and leave the rest of the arrangement intact.

<Callout type="warning" title="Recall yield first">
An asset with an open Lulo position fails with `FundsStillDeployed`. Recall the strategy,
then withdraw. For a boosted position, both phases of the withdrawal have to complete —
a pending withdrawal counts as exposure.
</Callout>

## Racing an heir

Once an estate is claimable, both you and the heir can sign. There is no priority rule
and no lock: whichever transaction lands first wins, and the second fails on an empty
vault or a closed account.

This is intentional. An estate that could be frozen by an heir's pending intent would
hand them a power the design does not want them to have.

## What you get back

Every registered asset's balance, minus 0.5% each, plus the rent from the estate, vault,
`AssetRecord` and token accounts as they close. The addresses become free to derive again
— see the note about RPC lag in [Create an estate](/docs/using/create-estate/).

<Callout type="danger" title="Withdrawing ends the arrangement">
Once the last asset is out, the estate is gone: settings, clock, guardian, hot signer, and
the heir's entitlement. Reopening means a new transaction, new rent, and a clock that
starts from zero.
</Callout>
