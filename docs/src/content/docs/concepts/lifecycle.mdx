An estate is always in exactly one state, and the state is derived — not stored. The app
computes it from the same fields the program checks, so a badge on your dashboard can
never disagree with what a transaction would do.

<Code caption="app/src/services/heirloom.ts">
```ts
computeEstateState({
  lastHeartbeat,
  heartbeatInterval,
  gracePeriod,
  pausedUntil,
  createdAt,
  vaultEmpty,
}); // → "active" | "grace" | "claimable" | "distributed"
```
</Code>

When an estate has never been checked in, the computation anchors on `created_at` instead
of `last_heartbeat`, so a fresh estate is active from the moment it exists.

## Active

`now < last_heartbeat + heartbeat_interval`

Normal operation. You can do everything: add assets, deposit, route yield, change the
interval, change the heir, withdraw. The heir can do nothing. A guardian can defer, though
there is no reason to yet.

## Grace

`last_heartbeat + heartbeat_interval <= now < claimable_at`

The interval has lapsed. Nothing has changed about what anyone is *allowed* to do — the
owner still has every power, the heir still has none — but the estate is now visibly at
risk and the app says so.

This is the window a guardian exists for. A deferral is only accepted before
`claimable_at`, so grace is the last moment anyone other than you can add time.

## Claimable

`now >= max(claimable_at, paused_until)`

The heir can sign a claim. Nothing has moved yet and nothing will move on its own — there
is no keeper, no automation, and nobody paid to act. An estate can sit claimable
indefinitely.

You have not lost anything at this point either. Your emergency withdraw still works, and
if you get there first the estate closes and the heir's claim fails on an empty vault.
Whoever signs first, wins.

<Callout type="note" title="A pause is a floor, not a state">
`paused_until` does not create a fifth state. It raises the claimable moment:
`max(claimable_at, paused_until)`. An estate deep into its grace period with a live pause
is not claimable until the pause expires, even if the grace period ran out days ago.
</Callout>

## Distributed

The vault is empty and the estate and vault accounts are closed.

This is terminal for that pair of addresses in their current incarnation, and it is what
every exit path converges on: a claim, an emergency withdraw, or the old half of a
change-of-heir migration. Rent from both accounts is returned as part of the transaction
that empties them.

The same wallet pair can open a new estate afterwards, though see the note about RPC lag
in [The estate](/docs/concepts/estate/#closing).

## The countdowns

The app exposes two, and they are what the dashboard's timers count:

| Countdown | Reaches zero at |
|---|---|
| `secondsUntilGrace` | `last_heartbeat + heartbeat_interval` |
| `secondsUntilClaimable` | `max(claimable_at, paused_until)` |

Both are recomputed from chain state on every poll — every 15 seconds, or every 5 while a
create is confirming — rather than ticked locally, so a clock that drifts in your browser
cannot make an estate look safer than it is.
