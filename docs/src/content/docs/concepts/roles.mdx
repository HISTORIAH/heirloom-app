Four roles, two of them optional. Each is a public key stored on the estate account, and
each is checked by the program on every instruction that names it.

## Authority

The wallet that opened the estate. It is stored as `authority` and it is a seed of the
estate PDA, so it can never be changed — an estate belongs to the key that created it for
its whole life.

The authority can:

- register assets and deposit into them
- send check-ins and edit the interval, grace period, pause duration, and label
- route balances into a yield strategy and recall them
- reassign the heir, which migrates the estate to a new PDA pair
- withdraw everything at any time

Losing this key is what [wallet recovery](/docs/using/wallet-recovery/) is for: an estate
whose heir is your own second wallet survives the loss of its authority.

## Heir

One wallet, stored as `heir`, also a seed of the PDA. It can do exactly one thing —
claim — and only once `claimable_at` has passed.

The heir has no read privileges to protect and no action to prepare. They do not need
your address, because the claim page scans the program for estates naming their wallet.
They do not need a document, because the entitlement is the chain state. They need to
know Heirloom exists.

<Callout type="warning" title="An heir cannot be a nickname">
The heir is a raw address. There is no name service resolution, no recovery if you type
it wrong, and no confirmation that the wallet is controlled by the person you meant. Paste
it from the wallet itself, and consider sending them a token first to prove the address
lands where you think it does.
</Callout>

## Guardian (`delegate`)

Optional. A wallet you name that may **defer the deadline once**, pushing `paused_until`
to `now + pause_duration`.

That is the entire power. A guardian cannot move funds, cannot see anything you cannot,
cannot change the heir, cannot alter a single duration, and cannot defer twice — the
program requires `paused_until == 0` on entry, so once used it stays used until you come
back and check in after it has expired.

<Code caption="programs/heirloom/src/instructions/defer.rs">
```rust
#[account(
    mut,
    address = estate.delegate.ok_or(HeirloomError::Unauthorized)?
        @ HeirloomError::Unauthorized
)]
pub delegate: Signer,
```
</Code>

Naming one is a real reduction in your own risk and costs you nothing in control. The
worst a hostile guardian can do is delay an inheritance by your own `pause_duration`.

## Hot signer (`hb_signer`)

Optional. A second wallet of yours that may send check-ins — and only check-ins.

The permission check accepts either the authority or the hot signer, but the settings
branch is gated separately on the signer being the authority:

<Code caption="programs/heirloom/src/instructions/update_field.rs">
```rust
estate.last_heartbeat = now;

if *authority_key == estate.authority {
    // interval / grace / pause_duration / label are applied here only
}
```
</Code>

A hot signer passing settings arguments therefore gets a heartbeat and nothing else — no
error, no partial write. The point is that a phone wallet can keep an estate alive without
being able to reshape it.

## Changing who is who

| Role | Changeable? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | No | It is a PDA seed. Open a new estate |
| Heir | Yes | [`update_heir`](/docs/using/change-heir/) migrates to a new PDA pair |
| Guardian | Not yet | Set once, at creation |
| Hot signer | Not yet | Set once, at creation |

<Callout type="warning" title="Guardian and hot signer are set once">
Both are written by `initialize` and carried across a change-of-heir migration unchanged.
No instruction replaces either on a live estate. To swap a guardian or retire a hot
signer today, [withdraw everything](/docs/using/emergency-withdraw/) and open a new
estate — the assets return to your wallet minus the 0.5% exit fee, and the new estate can
name whoever you want.
</Callout>
