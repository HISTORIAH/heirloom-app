Heirloom is an inheritance protocol on Solana. You put assets into an **estate**, you
check in on a schedule you choose, and if the check-ins stop, the heir you named can
claim what is inside. No custodian, no lawyer, and no shared seed phrase.

## The problem

In self-custody, the keys are the asset. Lose them and the balance is still visible and
permanently out of reach. Die with them and your family inherits an address they cannot
open.

The usual workarounds make things worse. Splitting a seed phrase turns each holder into
a risk. A custodian undoes the point of self-custody. A will cannot sign a transaction.

## How Heirloom solves it

Heirloom replaces the shared secret with a condition the chain can check: **has the
owner checked in recently?** While the answer is yes, nothing moves and only you can
touch the estate. Once it is no, and stays no through a grace period you chose, one
named wallet can claim the assets.

The estate is a program account with no private key. Only the program can move funds
out of it, and only under the rules you set when you opened it.

## What you can do with it

- **Leave assets to someone.** Name their wallet as the heir.
- **Recover your own wallet.** Name a second wallet of yours as the heir. See
  [Wallet recovery](/docs/using/wallet-recovery/).
- **Earn while you wait.** Route estate balances into Lulo lending and recall them any
  time. See [Earn yield](/docs/using/yield/).
- **Protect tokenized stocks.** See [Tokenized stocks](/docs/stocks/overview/).

<Callout type="warning" title="Devnet">
The program is audited and deployed to devnet. It is not on mainnet yet, so treat
balances as test balances. See [Addresses & clusters](/docs/reference/addresses/).
</Callout>
