Heirloom is a **Solana-native inheritance protocol**. You lock assets into an on-chain
estate that only you control, you check in on a schedule you set, and if the check-ins
ever stop, the heir you named can claim what is inside — on-chain, without a custodian,
a lawyer, or a shared seed phrase.

Nothing about that arrangement is held by a company. The estate is a program-derived
account. The rules are in the program. The only party that can move funds out of an
estate is the program itself, executing a condition you wrote when you opened it.

## The problem

Self-custody is the right call and it has one unsolved failure mode: **the keys are the
asset**. Lose them and the balance is still there, visibly, permanently, and out of
reach. Die with them and your family inherits a public key they cannot open.

The workarounds are all worse than the problem. Splitting a seed phrase between people
makes each of them a threat. Handing it to a custodian gives back the thing self-custody
was for. Writing an address into a will asks a court to compel an act only a private key
can perform.

Heirloom replaces the shared secret with a **condition the chain can check**: has this
wallet signed anything in the last N days? While the answer is yes, nothing moves and
nobody but you can touch the vault. When the answer becomes no, and stays no through a
grace period you chose, one named wallet — and only that wallet — can pull the assets
out.

## An estate, in one account

Everything a Heirloom estate knows about itself lives in one account, derived from your
wallet and your heir's.

<Code caption="An Estate account, decoded">
```txt
authority           7xKX…9Wm2     the wallet that opened it — yours
heir                4Bn8…Qp1a     the one wallet that can claim
heartbeat_interval  2592000       30 days, in seconds
grace_period        604800        7 more days after the interval lapses
last_heartbeat      1757260800    the check-in that reset the clock
pause_duration      2592000       how far a guardian may push the deadline
paused_until        0             not paused
delegate            Some(9fR2…)   the guardian, optional
hb_signer           Some(2dLp…)   a hot wallet allowed to check in, optional
claimable_assets    3             vault token accounts still open
label               "Family estate"
```
</Code>

The vault that actually holds the assets is a second account beside it. Both are PDAs:
no private key exists for either, so no key can ever be leaked, seized, or lost.

## What is inside

An estate holds **SOL and any SPL tokens** you register. Registering an asset creates a
marker account for that mint, which is what makes it claimable — the token account alone
is not enough, since anyone can create one of those for anyone.

Balances do not have to sit still while they wait. Estate funds can be routed into
[Lulo lending](/docs/using/yield/) and pulled back at any time, with principal and yield
returning to the estate itself. Heirloom takes 10% of the yield a strategy earns and
nothing at all from the principal.

## Four roles

| Role | Who it is | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | You, the owner | Everything: fund, check in, change settings and heir, route yield, withdraw early |
| **Heir** | One wallet you name | Claim the estate — and only once it is claimable |
| **Guardian** *(optional)* | A person you trust, stored as `delegate` | Push the deadline out once, by your `pause_duration`. Cannot move funds or change anything else |
| **Hot signer** *(optional)* | A second wallet of your own, stored as `hb_signer` | Send check-ins, so your cold key can stay cold. Nothing else |

The heir does not have to be another person. Point an estate at a **second wallet you
control** and Heirloom becomes wallet recovery: a lost key stops being the end of the
story. See [Wallet recovery](/docs/using/wallet-recovery/).
