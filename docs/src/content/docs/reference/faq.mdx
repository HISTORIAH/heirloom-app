## The basics

### Is this a legal will?

No. Heirloom is programmable self-custody continuity — it moves control of on-chain assets
from one key to another under a condition the chain can check. It is not a testamentary
instrument, it is not filed anywhere, and it does not interact with probate. Talk to a
lawyer about your estate; use this for your keys.

### Do I have to give anyone my seed phrase?

Never. Nothing in Heirloom requires sharing key material with anyone, including your heir
and your guardian. The only credentials involved are wallets people already control.

### Can Heirloom take my assets?

No. There is no admin instruction, no privileged address, and no upgrade path that moves
funds. The four roles the estate names are the only signers any instruction accepts, and
the treasury address is a compile-time constant that only receives fees.

### What if Heirloom the company disappears?

The program stays on-chain and keeps working. The app is a convenience layer over it —
the source is public, the client is generated from the IDL, and every instruction can be
built without the interface.

## Setting one up

### Can I have more than one heir?

Not in one estate. An estate has exactly one heir, and every registered asset goes to
that wallet. Open one estate per heir and fund each with what belongs there — they run
independently.

### Can I change my heir later?

Yes. [`update_heir`](/docs/using/change-heir/) migrates the whole estate to a new PDA
pair. Your clock, settings, guardian and hot signer carry over; assets never leave the
program.

### Can I change my guardian or hot signer?

Not on a live estate. Both are set at creation and carried across migrations unchanged.
Changing one means withdrawing and opening a new estate.

### What is the longest interval I can set?

365 days, for the interval, the grace period, and the pause duration each.

### What happens if I set a very short interval?

It works, and the estate becomes claimable that much sooner. Zero is legal — an estate
with a zero interval and zero grace is claimable immediately. Almost never what you want
outside testing.

## Living with it

### What happens if I forget to check in?

The interval lapses and the estate enters its grace period. Nothing has happened yet —
you still have every power, and a check-in resets the clock completely. Only after the
grace period also runs does the heir gain the ability to claim.

### Can I get my assets back?

Any time, in any state before distribution, including after the estate has become
claimable. [Emergency withdraw](/docs/using/emergency-withdraw/) returns everything to
your wallet minus 0.5%.

### Does depositing count as a check-in?

No. Only `update_field` resets `last_heartbeat`. Deposits, registrations and yield
operations leave the clock untouched.

### Can I automate my check-ins?

Yes — register a hot signer and run the call from a script. But an estate that a script
keeps alive forever never reaches its heir, which defeats the point. Tie the automation
to something only you can renew.

## Claiming

### How does my heir know what to do?

They connect their wallet to [app.heirlm.xyz/claim](https://app.heirlm.xyz/claim) and the
page finds every estate naming their address. They do not need your address, a document,
or a password. The one thing they cannot work out alone is that Heirloom exists — tell
them.

### What if my heir claims too early?

They cannot. The program rejects it with `NotYetClaimable` until
`now >= max(last_heartbeat + interval + grace, paused_until)`.

### What if we both sign at once?

Whoever lands first wins; the second transaction fails on an empty vault. There is no
lock and no priority rule, deliberately — an heir who could freeze your withdrawal would
have a power the design does not give them.

### Does anything happen automatically when the deadline passes?

No. There is no keeper and no automation. The estate becomes claimable and waits
indefinitely until the heir signs.

## Money

### What does it cost?

Opening, funding, checking in, deferring and changing your heir are free of protocol fees
— you pay only Solana network fees. Claims cost 0.75%, emergency withdrawals 0.5%, and
yield strategies 10% of the yield earned. See [Fees](/docs/concepts/fees/).

### Is the 10% taken from my deposit?

No. Only from the yield a strategy returned above the principal that went out. A position
that came back flat pays nothing.

### Why does my vault show slightly less than I deposited?

The rent-exempt reserve. A PDA has to hold a small SOL balance to exist; the app
subtracts it from the displayed claimable balance. It is returned when the estate closes.

## Practicalities

### Which tokens can I use?

SOL and SPL tokens, on both the classic Token program and Token-2022. NFTs are not
supported. Mints with an active transfer hook are rejected at deposit, and mints with a
freeze authority carry that issuer's freeze risk into the vault.

### Is it on mainnet?

Not yet. The program is deployed to devnet and has been independently audited. See
[Addresses & clusters](/docs/reference/addresses/).

### Can I use it to recover my own lost wallet?

Yes — that is [wallet recovery](/docs/using/wallet-recovery/). Name a second wallet you
control as the heir, and a lost key stops being permanent.

### Is any of this private?

No. Every address, duration, label and balance is public on-chain, and anyone can
correlate them. Do not write anything into a label you would not publish.
