## What the program guarantees

- **No admin.** There is no privileged instruction, no pause switch, and no address that
  can move funds outside the four roles the estate itself names.
- **No key to lose.** The estate, the vault, and every asset record are PDAs. No private
  key exists for any of them.
- **No custody.** Assets sit in an account the program signs for. Heirloom holds nothing.
- **Fixed fee terms.** The three fee rates and the treasury address are compile-time
  constants. Changing them means deploying a new program.
- **Deterministic entitlement.** An heir can claim exactly when
  `now >= max(last_heartbeat + interval + grace, paused_until)`, and never before.

The program is deployed to devnet and has been **independently audited**.

## What it does not guarantee

<Callout type="danger" title="Freeze authority">
If a registered mint's issuer freezes the vault's token account, that asset is stuck until
they unfreeze it. This includes most regulated stablecoins. It is a property of the token,
not of Heirloom, and no program design can route around it.
</Callout>

**Transfer hooks.** Transfers use plain `transfer_checked` with no hook accounts. A mint
with an active hook fails at deposit and never enters an estate. The residual risk is a
mint deposited hook-free whose authority later enables one, which would block exits for
that asset. Resolving hook accounts dynamically is a known fix, not yet built.

**Transfer fees.** Not handled specially. A fee-on-transfer mint means a smaller amount
arrives; nothing gets stuck.

**Deployed funds block an inheritance.** An asset with an open Lulo position cannot be
claimed, and only the authority can recall it. If an estate goes claimable with a live
position, those funds stay in the strategy until the owner's key acts. Recall your
positions if you stop using an estate actively.

**External protocol risk.** Routing into Lulo means accepting Lulo's risk, and Lulo's
Kamino exposure, for as long as the position is open. Heirloom does not insure it.

**Nothing happens automatically.** There is no keeper. An estate that becomes claimable
stays claimable until an heir signs. If your heir does not know Heirloom exists, nothing
in this protocol will tell them.

## What is public

Everything on-chain, which is the whole arrangement: your address, your heir's address,
your guardian's address, your durations, your label, and every balance and transaction.
Anyone can read it and anyone can correlate it.

That is a property of Solana, not a Heirloom decision, but it is worth stating plainly
before you write a name into a label.

## What the app collects

Analytics are **off unless explicitly enabled** at build time, and even then the
integration is deliberately narrow. It records page views and clicks on links and
buttons. It does not identify wallet owners and does not send wallet addresses, heir
addresses, transaction signatures, asset amounts, form values, or error messages.
Autocaptured text is masked and session recording is disabled.

The app carries `noindex, nofollow` on every route and a `robots.txt` disallowing
everything, because there is nothing on it a crawler should hold.

## Operational advice

<Cards>
  <Card title="Use a hot signer" icon="smartphone">
    Keep the owning key cold. A hot signer can only ever send check-ins.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Name a guardian" icon="shield">
    The worst a hostile one can do is delay by your own pause duration, once.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Verify the heir address" icon="scan-line">
    No name resolution, no undo. Send a small transfer first.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Recall before you go quiet" icon="trending-down">
    Deployed funds are the one thing your heir cannot fix.
  </Card>
</Cards>

## Reporting a vulnerability

Email **info@heirlm.xyz** or DM **@heirloom_app**. The program publishes the same contact
in an on-chain `security_txt` block, with the repository as its policy URL.

Please do not open a public issue for anything that could put funds at risk.
