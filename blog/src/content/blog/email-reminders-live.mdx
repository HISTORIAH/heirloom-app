When we [launched reminders](/blog/reminders-live/) with Telegram, we said email was next. It's live today. You can get check-in reminders in your inbox, and your heir can be told by email when an estate becomes claimable. No app or bot needed on their end.

## Why email

Telegram works well if you already use it, but plenty of people don't. Everyone has an email address. That matters even more for your heir, who may never have heard of Heirloom and might not know what a wallet is. An email is something they already know how to open.

## Confirming your address

Add your email under **Notifications** and we'll send a confirmation with an 8-character code and a one-tap link. Enter the code or click the link, and reminders are on. Codes leave out characters that look alike (no O, 0, I or 1), so you can read one off your phone and type it on your laptop without guessing.

![The confirmation email: "Is this you?" with an 8-character one-time code and a Confirm my email button](/blog/images/email-verify.png)

Clicking the link still asks you to sign in with the wallet that owns the estate. Someone who gets hold of the email can't confirm it for you.

Once you're confirmed, we send one short email that says which estate it covers and where your heir's alert will go.

![The "You're plugged in" email, showing where heir alerts will go and when you'll hear from us](/blog/images/email-connected.png)

## What lands in your inbox

The schedule is the same as on Telegram: a week before your check-in is due, when it's missed, through the grace period, and 24 hours before it ends. Check in at any point and the rest of that cycle's reminders are cancelled.

![The "Quick check-in?" email, sent 7 days before a check-in is due](/blog/images/email-due-soon.png)

If a check-in is missed, the email shows where you are in the grace period and the last day you can still check in. Until then, nothing changes.

![The "We didn't hear from you" email, showing the missed date, today, and the date the heir can claim](/blog/images/email-grace-started.png)

Each email does one job. It tells you the date, links to check in, and says what happens if you don't. That's it: no newsletters, no "we miss you" emails.

![The "Still here. Noted." email confirming a check-in and showing the next due date](/blog/images/email-check-in-recorded.png)

## An email your heir will trust

The heir alert is the most important email we send, and the easiest one to mistake for a scam. "Someone left you crypto, click here" is exactly what a phishing email looks like. So we let you shape it:

- **Their name**, so it's addressed to them.
- **What they call you.** "Mum" or "Michael" means something to them. A wallet address doesn't.
- **A personal note**, in your own words, up to 500 characters.

![The heir email: "Michael left something for you", with a personal note, three steps to claim, and a staying-safe box](/blog/images/email-heir.png)

The alert goes out an hour after the grace period ends, and never before. Your heir hears nothing from us while you're still checking in. It's written for someone with no crypto experience, and it tells them plainly that Heirloom will never ask for a seed phrase, a password or a payment.

We don't send your heir a verification code, because confirming it would mean emailing them before anything has happened. That makes the address yours to get right, so the app asks you to type it twice.

## How to turn it on

1. Open the [app](https://app.heirlm.xyz) and find your estate on the dashboard
2. Under **Notifications**, click **Set up**
3. Sign the message to prove you own the wallet. No transaction, no gas
4. Choose **Email** and enter your address
5. Optionally add your heir's email, their name, what they call you, and a note
6. Save, then enter the code from your inbox or click the link in it

You can use email and Telegram on the same estate. Check the spelling before you save, because contacts can't be changed or removed yet. That's coming.

## Your contact details

Email addresses get the same treatment as Telegram usernames. Collection is opt-in per estate, details are encrypted at rest and only decrypted when a message is sent, settings are gated by your wallet's signature, and nothing touches the chain.
