The hardest part of crypto inheritance isn't the smart contract. It's the heir. Most heirs don't have a wallet, don't want a seed phrase, and won't remember a password they were given years ago.

So the Heirloom mobile app lets you name a card as your heir instead of a wallet address. When the time comes, your heir taps the card on their phone to claim. Starting today, that card can be a Tangem card.

## Why Tangem

Plenty of people already keep their crypto on a Tangem card, and others are buying one for a parent or a partner. If your heir already has one, they shouldn't need a second card just for Heirloom.

A Tangem card is also a good fit for inheritance for the same reason it's a good wallet. The private key is created on the chip and never leaves it. Nobody can copy it: not you, not your heir, and not us.

## How it works

### Setting it up

When you create an estate in the app:

1. Under **Who inherits this estate?**, choose **Give them an Heirloom credential**
2. Pick **Tangem card**
3. Tap the card on your phone. Tangem's own sheet opens and reads the card

We read the card's Solana address, the same address the Tangem app shows. The card has to be activated already, so if it's new, set it up in the Tangem app first.

That address is your heir. The rest of the estate works the same as always: choose what to leave, set your check-in interval, and keep checking in.

### Handing it over

Give your heir the card, and give them its access code separately. Don't tape the code to the card. The app gives you a short note to send them, so they know what the card is for and what to do with it.

The card stays an ordinary Tangem wallet. Your heir can keep it in a drawer, or use it every day. Neither affects the estate.

### Claiming

If you stop checking in and the grace period ends, your heir opens the Heirloom app and taps their card. The app sees that it's a Tangem card and passes it to Tangem's sheet, which asks for the access code. One tap signs the whole claim, and the funds go to the card's Solana address.

After that, they're in your heir's Tangem wallet, the same one they see in the Tangem app. There's no new wallet to set up and no seed phrase to write down.

## What we never see

Heirloom never holds the card's key, and it can't sign anything without the card and its access code. Every signature the card returns is checked against the address you registered before anything is sent. If someone taps the wrong card, the app says so instead of submitting a transaction that would fail.

## Heirloom cards, too

Tangem cards aren't the only option. The app also sets up blank Heirloom cards, rings and bands, creating the key on the chip during setup. Your heir can either tap once to claim or keep the card as a PIN-protected wallet. We'll write about them separately.

## Availability

The mobile app runs on Android and is on devnet for now, so it can't hold real funds yet. The dApp Store release and mainnet are next.
